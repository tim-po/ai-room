## Learning evidence and chosen implementation implications

**S01 — retrieval, controlled evidence.** [Roediger & Karpicke 2006, original paper](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2006/2006_Roediger_Karpicke_PsychSci.pdf): experiments with prose and recall found an immediate advantage for restudy but a delayed advantage for prior testing. Evidence is strong for the tested memory tasks; transfer to practical AI ability and platform return is unproven. D06: offer a short attempt before revealing a reviewed explanation; do not substitute rereading or confidence for evidence. Acceptance: reviewed item, response, source-linked explanation and later review can be followed without asserting applied competence. Counterexample: an immediate quiz score is not delayed learning, and a remembered answer is not a novel project solution.

**S02 — spacing, controlled evidence.** [Cepeda et al. 2008, author manuscript](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0kp5q19x/qt0kp5q19x_noSplash_aedacd613d87c6d68878ac7ba09fdf0b.pdf): study of fact learning over varying review gaps and retention delays; suitable spacing depends on the desired retention interval. Strong evidence for spacing in this domain, weak basis for any universal daily rule. D07: optional review of previously taught/reviewed material and a normal return-after-break state; no streak loss. Acceptance: completed content can reappear as review without erasing original completion; unsupported review inventory has an honest unavailable state. Counterexample: fixed 24-hour reminders or a “scientifically optimal” schedule are not justified by this study.

**S03 — corrective feedback, controlled evidence with access caveat.** [Butler & Roediger 2008, author institution abstract](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/feedback-enhances-the-positive-effects-and-reduces-the-negative-e/) compares immediate/delayed/no feedback after multiple-choice testing; feedback improved later recall and reduced intrusion of distractors. Full author PDF timed out in this session, so this assessment uses the institutional abstract. D08: wrong/partial answer gets a reviewed explanation and source, plus a relevant next step. Counterexample: “incorrect” alone can leave misconceptions intact; neither this experiment nor a vendor demo proves that instantaneous AI-generated feedback is trustworthy or best in every setting.

These papers concern **retention of learned information**, not D1/D7 product retention. They provide a reason to test useful learning features, not a license to promise business uplift.
